BASE MAP FROM A PICTURE (Sprout Lands game)

SETTINGS (you can change these two lines)
- Map width: 60 columns. Use 40, 50, 60 or 70. (40 suits a small scene such as one garden or clearing; 60 suits an island.)
- Map name: pick a short name that fits the picture.

YOUR JOB
The picture I attached is a top-down map. Redraw its layout as a text map for my kids' pixel-art game. Each character is one square 16 x 16 ground tile, about the size of the player. Draw the BASE only: water, grass, sand paths, bushes, hills and bridges. No buildings and no objects. I will add trees, flowers, animals and buildings myself later.

Map height: keep the picture's shape. Height = width x (picture height / picture width), rounded to a whole number. A square picture at width 60 gives 60 rows. Never more than 90 rows; if it would be, use a smaller width.

USE ONLY THESE 9 CHARACTERS
~  water: sea, rivers, ponds, lakes
.  grass
:  sand: dirt paths, beaches, bare earth, sandy yards, farm soil
b  bushes: thick hedge walls and dense forest (nobody can walk through)
h  hill: a block of raised grass with a short edge all round
^  stairs up onto a hill
=  wooden bridge going left-right
|  wooden bridge or pier going up-down
o  stepping stones in water
Spaces only go between the groups of 10 (see ANSWER FORMAT). Never use any other character, and never use a space as a tile.

WHAT THE GAME LOOKS LIKE (translate the picture into this)
The game uses Cup Nooble's "Sprout Lands" tiles: flat top-down ground, soft green grass that meets the water with a soft edge, pale teal water, tan sand paths with soft edges, round leafy bush walls, and low grass hills with a short edge. The tiles cannot make cliffs, stone walls, rocky rims, dark soil banks, or deep and shallow water.
- A grey stone or rocky rim around the land -> an ordinary coast: grass (or a sand beach) right next to ~
- A cliff, ledge or raised plateau inside the land -> h hill
- Dense forest, a hedge, a wall of bushes, a thick leafy border round the map -> b
- A dirt road, trail, sandy yard, beach or farm field -> :
- Sea, river, pond or lake -> ~ (light and dark water are both ~)
- A wooden bridge or boardwalk -> = or |. A pier or jetty -> | (or =) sticking out into the water.
- Stepping stones in water -> o
- Single trees, trees with grass between them, rocks, flowers, tall grass, mushrooms, stumps, logs, crates, hay, barrels, chests, signs, animals, lily pads, rocks in the water -> leave them out and draw the ground under them (usually . or ~).
- Houses, barns, coops, sheds, wells, fences, gates, walls, crops, market stalls -> leave them out. Draw plain ground where they stood (. or :), the same size, so the space stays free. List them under NOTES.
- Titles, logos, text, buttons, watermarks -> ignore them and continue the ground around them.

RULES FOR A CLEAN MAP
1. Exact size: exactly [height] rows, and every row exactly [width] characters.
2. Keep the picture's layout: the coastline shape, water, paths, forest, hills and open spaces all stay in the same places.
3. Nature is never straight. Coasts, river banks, pond edges, forest edges and hill edges step in or out every 3 to 6 tiles. Only built things (fields, yards, paths beside buildings, bridges) may be straight.
4. No crumbs. No lone tiles: no single water tile in grass, single grass tile in water, single bush, or one-tile peninsula. Every patch of water, land, bushes or hill is at least 2 tiles wide at every point. Things that join must share a side, not just touch at a corner.
5. Paths (:) are 1 tile wide; they look wider in the game. Use 2 wide only for a main road. Paths bend and join places together.
6. Ponds are at least 5 tiles across, uneven ovals, with at least 2 tiles of land between a pond and the sea. Rivers are at least 3 tiles wide.
7. Bushes (b): walls at least 2 tiles thick, forest at least 5 thick. Forest along the map edge works well.
8. Hills (h): at least 5 x 5, a solid block of h with rounded, uneven corners, and at least 1 tile of grass or sand all round (a hill never touches water or bushes). Nothing else inside a hill. A hill's outer ring can't be walked on, so every hill needs stairs: 2 ^ side by side on its bottom (south) edge, with grass or a path right below them.
9. Bridges are straight, 1 tile wide, at least 2 tiles long, and only over water. = runs left-right, | runs up-down. Every bridge starts on land and ends on land (or runs off the map edge, if it does in the picture); only a pier may end in the water.
10. Stepping stones: a short straight line of 2 to 4 o over narrow water, with land at both ends.
11. The player must be able to walk everywhere: all land joins up (through bridges or stepping stones), unless the picture clearly shows an island with no way to it.
12. Edges: if the picture's land stops before its edge, leave at least 2 tiles of water round the whole map. If land runs off the picture's edge, let it touch the map edge.

HOW TO WORK
Lay an imaginary grid of [width] x [height] squares over the picture. Place the big shapes first (coast, water, forest, hills), then the paths, then bridges and stepping stones. Fill the rows one at a time, top to bottom.
